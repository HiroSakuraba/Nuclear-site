American Nuclear Power for Puerto Rico

Replace retiring oil and coal with U.S.-designed reactors at two sites, and give the island firm power that stays safe through a Category 5 hurricane.

1.1–1.4 GWfirm, always-on power: 37–47% of island peak demand

The problem

Nine years after Hurricane Maria, one plant trip can still cut power to nearly 150,000 customers. Two-thirds of the island's generation sits on the south coast, but most people live in the north. After Maria, that power could not cross the mountains. Meanwhile, the island's largest oil plant and its only coal plant are scheduled to close.

The plan

Aguirre, Salinas

600–900 MW

2–3 GE Vernova BWRX-300 reactors replace the 1,492 MW oil-burning complex, reusing its grid hub, land, and nearby port.

Roosevelt Roads, Ceiba

462 MW

6 NuScale modules on the San Juan side of the mountains, next to the reactivated military airfield and deep-water pier.

Schematic. Ceiba reaches San Juan without crossing the Cordillera Central.

Why it fits the President's agenda

  • Delivers on the May 2025 nuclear executive orders: 400 GW by 2050, faster NRC licensing, and reactors for defense and critical infrastructure.
  • Energy dominance: it replaces imported fuel oil with U.S. nuclear technology, not Russian or Chinese reactors.
  • National security: firm power in the Caribbean next to the reactivated Roosevelt Roads airfield and port.
  • Fixes what nine years of rebuilding has not: a grid that still fails.
  • Builds on a proven path: the same BWRX-300 is under construction in Ontario, and the NRC staff recommended a U.S. construction permit for it in 2026.

The ask: wins this term

  1. Direct DOE to fund site studies at Aguirre and Roosevelt Roads.
  2. Name it a priority project under the nuclear executive orders and the National Energy Dominance Council.
  3. Partner with Governor González Colón. She wants power that is "reliable, not intermittent." This delivers it, and she shares the credit.
  4. File Early Site Permits before January 2029, using the Tennessee Valley Authority's Clinch River SMR project as the template.

Announcement-ready: a decision, a funded study, and permits filed, all within this term.

The honest numbers

  • Cost: at least $17–21B, based on comparable projects in Idaho and Ontario.
  • Financing: private developers, DOE loans, and long-term power contracts. Not a PREPA bailout.
  • Timeline: 2–3 years of studies, then licensing and construction. First power in the mid-2030s.
  • Hurdles: the Governor's current position against nuclear, community buy-in in Salinas and Guayama, the Jobos Bay reserve, NRC population rules, and interim power until the reactors come online.

Why these reactors

Both shut down and cool themselves with no pumps, outside power, or operators, which matters most on an island that can be cut off for weeks. They are small enough for the grid to absorb a trip, and they run on standard fuel.
