# Siting Small Modular Reactors in Puerto Rico

**A two-site option at Aguirre and Ceiba**

Benjamin John Schulz · Independent research · Revision 3, September 2026

---

This repository holds a planning-level policy analysis. It asks whether small modular reactors (SMRs) could give Puerto Rico firm, hurricane-resilient power, where they should go, and which designs fit the island's grid. It also includes a one-page pitch for decision-makers.

## Recommendation in brief

| Site | Technology | Net capacity | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aguirre, Salinas** | 2–3 × GE Vernova Hitachi BWRX-300 | 600–900 MWe | Replaces the retiring 1,492 MW oil-fired complex at an existing grid hub, using seawater cooling and a nearby port |
| **Ceiba (Roosevelt Roads)** | 1 × NuScale US460 (6 × 77 MWe) | 462 MWe | Firm supply on the San Juan side of the Cordillera Central; small modules suit the weaker eastern grid |
| **Total** | | **1,062–1,362 MWe** | About 37–47% of island peak demand |

Both sites use seawater for cooling. The safety structures sit on raised ground set back from surge and tsunami. According to the vendors, both designs remove decay heat without pumps, AC power, or operator action.

**Indicative cost:** at least $17–21 billion, scaled from comparable projects. **First power:** mid-2030s at the earliest.

**Recommended next step:** fund a two-to-three-year site characterization program at both sites before any construction commitment.

## Limitations

This is a planning-level analysis based on public sources. It is not an engineering design, cost estimate, site characterization, or licensing submission. Vendor performance claims, including passive-cooling duration and black-start capability, are unverified until confirmed through NRC review. The paper does not represent the position of any government entity.
